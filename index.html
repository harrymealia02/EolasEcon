Is the everyday Irish economy actually growing?

The short answer: Headline GDP swings wildly due to multinational accounting. Modified Domestic Demand (MDD) shows steady, moderate domestic expansion.

The short answer: After losing ground during the 2022–2023 inflation spike, average weekly earnings have begun growing faster than consumer prices again.

Source: CSO Earnings & Labour Costs / CPI (Annual % change)

Why EolasEcon ignores headline Irish GDP

In almost every other country, Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is a reliable shorthand for economic health. In Ireland, aircraft leasing, contract manufacturing, and intellectual property transfers by large multinationals routinely distort GDP by tens of billions of euro—creating paper booms or slumps that ordinary households never feel.

Instead, EolasEcon tracks Modified Domestic Demand (MDD), real take-home pay, physical housing completions, and grid delivery—the metrics that actually reflect living standards and state capacity on the ground.
